Work

Seven years at Network Ninja, maker of MainEvent field marketing software, and what I've built since.

AI adoption · Network Ninja · 2025

Getting clients to use an AI product well

The company launched an AI agent that answers questions about field marketing data in plain language. I owned every client's adoption of it. When I started there was no onboarding process, no training and no way to test the answers.

I tested the agent the way a client would use it, wrote down where it went wrong, and fed that back to the product team before clients saw it.

What I built

  • A manual testing framework that caught hallucinations before they reached clients
  • A five-phase onboarding method: discovery, implementation, testing and validation, training, ongoing support
  • Training at four levels: everyday users, power users, executives, and our own account managers
  • A library of 75+ tested prompt templates

Independent build · 2026

Parallax

A sales-enablement app I designed and directed. It takes a product profile and what's known about a prospect, and writes a prospect-specific brief, demo talking points, a demo script, a one-pager and discovery questions.

It's built around one reason deals stall: buyers can't picture the product in their own workflow. Outputs mark what's confirmed apart from what's inferred, and say what the product doesn't solve. Discovery questions are written without seeing the brief, so they go looking for what it missed.

Phase 1 launched September 2026. React, the Claude API and Vercel, with a 77-test offline suite.

About

I notice things that aren't being said. Systems failing quietly. People overwhelmed and covering well. Two teams talking past each other, both convinced the other one is the problem. I notice, and then I do something about it.

My dad was a house painter. I grew up understanding that doing something properly matters, even when nobody's watching. Especially then.

Philosophy degree from Guelph. Hermeneutics specifically, which is the study of how meaning is made and lost between people. Social work training from George Brown. Then a receptionist job that turned into accounts receivable, London for a bit, sales ops, and eventually seven-plus years at a startup that became about five different jobs. None of it was a plan. All of it was useful.
